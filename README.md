## The entry

> They will decide what your AI may say, yet the lawmaker writing the rules still emails his draft to an aide with the subject line 'please PDF this for me'.

155 characters, one sentence, one roast.

## Why this line

1. **One-sentence satirical roast** — a single sentence roasting a lawmaker who would regulate AI while being unable to convert a file to PDF.
2. **A real turn** — the setup grants sweeping authority ("decide what your AI may say"), then undercuts it with the file he cannot convert: his own draft, emailed to an aide to be turned into a PDF.
3. **Aim on lawmakers and the rules they write** — the target is power and competence (deciding what AI may say vs. not being able to operate the tool that carries the rule), not random abuse.
4. **Clean in one pass, quotable** — plain words, no unexplained reference, and the punch lands on the last few words: the subject line 'please PDF this for me'.
5. **A specific, unexpected detail** — not the worn "can't use a computer" cliche, but a concrete artefact: the lawmaker's own draft, emailed to an aide with a helpless subject line. The bill itself is the file he cannot convert.

Seven candidate lines were written, each scored 1–5 on the five judging criteria, then the best went through two improvement passes (tightening wording, cutting bytes — the tie-break favours fewer bytes). Pass 1 dropped the period inside the quoted subject line (keeping it strictly one sentence) and changed "forwards" to "emails" (167 → 165 chars); pass 2 changed "is allowed to say" to "may say" and "but" to "yet" (165 → 155 chars).
